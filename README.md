# theORQL Community

**Turn a market chart into research you can defend.**

theORQL is a financial research platform for independent analysts, research publishers, and investment teams. Compare what moved, look for evidence that could change your view, and show how you reached your conclusion. Our aim is to make quantitative research easier to do while keeping the data, rules, and results open to inspection.

[Open theORQL Research](https://app.theorql.com/) · [Join the community discussion](https://github.com/the-ORQL/community/discussions)

## What you can investigate

### See which markets are leading and where they diverge

A comparison means more when both series use the same dates and a clearly stated way of measuring returns. Chart supported market series side by side, then use relationship studies to see whether they have been moving together. Inspect the observations behind the calculation so you can explain what the comparison shows.

### Find the evidence that could change your mind

A rally in the headline index can hide weakness elsewhere. Ask whether the average stock is keeping up, whether participation extends across sectors, or whether a different time window tells a different story. Put evidence for and against your view side by side. When a finding suggests a new rule worth testing, keep it as a separate question so you can compare it with the original.

### Find out what followed comparable conditions

Define what counts as a match, then look at the dates that qualify and what followed over a stated period. In supported historical studies, the matching events, sample size, and range of outcomes give you more to work with than an average alone. You can see whether the result was consistent across episodes or driven by a handful of unusual cases.

### Show your work and pick it up again later

Keep the settings and evidence behind a supported study with its saved result. When you return to a question or explain it to a colleague, you can inspect the inputs and results without reconstructing the calculation from a chart or a note.

## An example to try with your own research question

**Is the rally reaching the average stock?** Start by comparing an equal-weight basket with a capitalization-weighted benchmark over the same period. Relative performance tells you which series led. A rolling correlation of daily returns answers a different question: whether the two series have been moving together. If the comparison changes your view, state what evidence would make you revise it again. If you want to test what followed similar divergences in the past, write down the condition and the outcome period before looking at the results.

## Help shape the first Excel workflow

We are developing a workflow that starts with a workbook you already use, turns a selected series into a chart, and lets you inspect a comparison or calculation from there. If you already do this in Excel, share a question you answer regularly and tell us which step takes the most manual work. A description of your process is enough to start the discussion.
